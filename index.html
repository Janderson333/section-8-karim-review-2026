What I Wish I Knew Before Starting

This is the honest part of the review.

Here's what I struggled with:

The Learning Curve Is Real

Section 8 rules vary by city and county.

What works in Cleveland might not work in Columbus.

I spent hours on the phone with different housing authorities, and it was frustrating at times. The program helps, but you still have to do the work.

Capital Is Non-Negotiable

You need money to buy properties and make repairs.

If you're starting with zero capital, this program won't magically solve that.

I started with about $40,000 saved, and I leveraged it carefully.

It Takes Time

My first deal took four months from joining the program to getting a lease signed.

The process speeds up once you learn the system, but the beginning can be slow.

If you need fast money, this isn't it.

Time Commitment

Education alone doesn't acquire properties.

Students still need to research markets, analyze deals, secure financing, make offers, complete inspections, and manage the acquisition process.

For most students, committing approximately 3–5 focused hours per week has been a solid benchmark for staying engaged with the training, analyzing opportunities, and consistently taking action.

Individual results and time requirements will vary depending on experience, market, and investment goals.

Repairs Are More Expensive Than Expected

Even with the program's cost estimators, I underestimated renovation costs on my first two properties.

My coach helped me refine my budgeting, but I lost some money in the beginning.
